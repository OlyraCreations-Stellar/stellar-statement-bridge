## Infrastructure and adoption constraints

- Ledger events are not business intent. Matching a chain payment to an invoice or payout requires reliable identifiers, asset issuer/address, amount semantics, timestamps/ledger, and a human-reviewed exception policy.
- Public ledger transparency creates privacy concerns if business counterparties, invoice references, or customer identities are placed on-chain. Keep personal/business data off-chain and minimize retained data.
- Horizon and RPC availability, rate limits, ingestion windows, pagination, and data consistency must be treated as dependencies. An operator product needs retry, replay, deduplication, and explicit “unknown/pending” states.
- A fiat payout product also depends on anchors, liquidity, banks/mobile money operators, corridor pricing, KYC/AML responsibility, and local legal rules. Stellar settlement does not supply these automatically.
- Smart contracts add value only if the product needs enforceable escrow/authorization or shared state. A read-only index/matching MVP should start off-chain and use established Stellar APIs.
- A new app faces incumbent feature overlap and the cold-start problem. A pilot operator with real exception data matters more than a broader feature list.

## How to interpret overlap

Similarity is not a rejection rule. It is a map of what users can already do and where a proposed improvement must earn its place. A new project may be worthwhile when it makes an existing workflow materially easier, safer, more accessible, or more complete. The relevant question is whether the improvement solves a demonstrated user problem better than extending or integrating with the existing option.

The full public Drips catalog was scanned across 842 repository records using names, descriptions, and tags. Those records were grouped for broad category discovery; keyword totals are not counts of verified capabilities. Detailed workflow comparisons were then made for the most relevant repositories and official SDP documentation. This is not a source-code audit of all 842 projects. Catalog-wide similarity labels are therefore provisional, and the deeper comparisons are explicitly limited to the linked candidates.

## Current conclusion

The current direction should improve the existing SDP workflow rather than recreate its payout sender or dashboard. SDP accepts custom payment IDs and provides searchable payment status and exports. PaymentFlow and Concord show related reconciliation patterns in school-fee and securities-register domains; DeenBridge and TrustBridge address other parts of payment verification/readiness. These similarities suggest useful design patterns and raise the differentiation bar; they do not make an ERP integration an automatic duplicate.

A meaningful step beyond the current tools could be a focused ERPNext app: fetch paginated SDP payment records, validate `SUCCESS` status, Stellar transaction ID and exact asset issuer mapping, then import statement-side Bank Transactions with unique source links. ERPNext's native reconciliation can match those records to vouchers; the app must not post vouchers or move money. This is a tighter, testable improvement than a generic matcher, though customer demand and existing connectors remain unverified. Consider contributing upstream if the better route is to extend an existing project. Do not create a competing product just to have a separate repo. Final product and organization names remain undecided.
